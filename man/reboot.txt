NAME
       reboot, halt, poweroff, sync - restart or stop the system

SYNOPSIS
       reboot [-fn] [-d DELAY]
       halt [-fn] [-d DELAY]
       poweroff [-fn] [-d DELAY]
       sync

DESCRIPTION
       reboot restarts the board as the reset button does: every core and
       the peripherals, back through the boot ROM and yasboot.

       halt stops every core and leaves them stopped until the next reset.

       poweroff switches off as much as the chip can. On an RP2350 that is
       POWMAN's deepest state: the cores, SRAM and the XIP cache lose power
       and only the always-on domain keeps running, waiting for a reset
       (the RUN pin or a button on it, a debugger's rescue, a power cycle).
       The board's regulator, PSRAM and SD card stay powered: they are not
       the chip's to switch. A board with nothing deeper, halts. With a
       debug probe connected over SWD the chip stays powered and halts
       instead: the probe's power-up request is honoured, so the debugger
       can always reset it, and a probe connected later wakes a chip that
       did power down.

       Under QEMU, halt and poweroff exit the emulator with status 0, and
       reboot resets the machine.

       All three first write out what the mounted filesystems hold back,
       as sync does, and close the kernel log on /var/log. The kernel does
       this itself in reboot(2), so it happens even with -n. What they do
       not do is stop other processes first: YasOS has no init to ask
       them. A file another program still has open can lose what that
       program has not written yet, as at a power cut. Close it, or quit
       the program, first.

       sync writes out the filesystems' buffered blocks: lwext4's block
       cache, the FAT driver's write-combining buffer, the kernel log.
       Files a process has open keep their own buffers until fsync(2) or
       close(2).

OPTIONS
       -d DELAY
              Wait DELAY seconds first (m, h and d suffixes: -d 1.5m is 90
              seconds).

       -f     Accepted. Linux's reboot signals init without it; YasOS always
              calls reboot(2) directly.

       -n     Do not call sync(2) first. The kernel syncs anyway.

EXAMPLES
       poweroff
              Before pulling the power on a Pico Plus 2 with an SD card.

       reboot -d 5
              Restart in five seconds.

SEE ALSO
       sys/reboot.h (reboot(2)), unistd.h (sync(2))

NOTES
       On QEMU (mps3-an524) the second core does not come back after a
       reset; the rebooted guest runs on core 0 only. QEMU's own
       system_reset does the same.
